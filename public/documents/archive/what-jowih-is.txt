What Jowih Is

Jowih is a development-focused organization designed to complement the African Union and national governments by providing execution capacity in areas where frameworks and policies already exist but require structured implementation support.

The African Union operates primarily as a political,development and policy body. It sets frameworks, coordinates member states, and represents the continent on the global stage. These are essential functions. They provide the direction and legitimacy that continental development requires.

What Africa has often needed alongside these frameworks is a partner dedicated specifically to execution — an organization that can work with the AU, national governments, and the private sector to help translate continental frameworks like AfCFTA and Agenda 2063 into tangible projects and measurable outcomes. It does this by reinvesting 90% of its revenue to developments and 54 country pots yearly.

Jowih is designed to serve that complementary role. It does not replace the AU. It does not replace national governments. It works alongside them — mobilizing capital, coordinating delivery partners, and building the infrastructure and systems through which shared development goals can be realized.

That is what Jowih is. Not a competitor. A partner in execution.

How Jowih Works With National Governments

Jowih works with sovereign African states through the Accelerated Development Framework Agreement (ADFA).

ADFA is a bilateral or multilateral agreement with the State as a perpetual legal entity — not with any particular government, administration, ministry, or official. It endures beyond elections, coups, revolutions, and constitutional reforms. The Foundation invests in nations, not administrations.

ADFA is structured for 20-year cycles, renewed automatically on agreements, aligned with the Jowih 2045 Horizon.

ADFA provides three execution models:

[[TABLE]]
| Model | Who Funds | Who Owns |
| Model A — State-Funded | The participating state | The state |
| Model B — Foundation-Funded | The Jowih Foundation | The Foundation under Asset Lock |
| Model C — Joint-Funded | State and Foundation jointly | Per agreed structure |
[[/TABLE]]

Each country participates according to its own priorities, fiscal capacity, and legal requirements.

How Jowih Is Funded

The initial capital comes from the African diaspora and Africans at large.

The African diaspora represents a combined net worth of approximately US$1 trillion. This is one of the largest pools of African capital in the world. It is also one of the least structured when it comes to long-term continental development.

The diaspora also sends over US$100 billion in remittances to Africa every year. Most of it is consumed. Some of it is invested. But none of it is structured as long-term development capital.

Jowih's proposition is simple: if the diaspora can be shown a credible, well-governed, long-term development architecture, they may be willing to invest roughly US$100 billion into the Grid Bond.

The Grid Bond is a one-time activation capital instrument. It is designed to fund:

Strategic acquisitions

Strategic stakes in global companies per corridor

Technology transfer and licenses.

Exchange programs and workforce training

Research and Development Centers

Major STEM university

Initial Phase of Propose New Dawn City. The economic powerhouse of Africa. Shenzhen capabilities.

The initial phase of the Great African Grid

Initial Country Pot allocations for development

The Grid Bond

The Grid Bond is structured with interest payments (5.0 - 5.4 )deferred for the first four-five years.

During those five years, Jowih focuses on building and revenue creation.

Meanwhile, the Mission Fund continues to receive revenue from acquisitions, stakes, new companies, and ecosystem activity. By Year 5, the Mission Fund is projected to receive approximately US$44 billion per year across its ecosystem in the continent and globally.

That is more than enough to cover the first interest payments on the Grid Bond by a wide margin.

The Jowih Ecosystem Fund

The Grid Bond builds the foundation. The Jowih Ecosystem Fund is participation in the commercial ecosystem that follows.

The Jowih Ecosystem Fund is the regulated investment vehicle through which African citizens and the diaspora participate economically in eligible Jowih commercial enterprises. It is organized around the Jowih 100 → 500 Index — the definitive index of the top eligible companies in Africa, including Jowih's own operating companies and qualified ecosystem partners.

[[TABLE]]
| Bucket | Weight | Composition |
| Jowih Core | 30% | Qualified Jowih Group companies and Jowih-origin commercial enterprises |
| Ecosystem Partners | 70% | Qualified third-party African enterprises across logistics, energy, fintech, agritech, utilities, infrastructure, manufacturing, healthcare, and education |
[[/TABLE]]

The Fund vaults qualifying assets, issues regulated non-voting investment units, and provides NAV exposure to the Index.

What the Ecosystem Fund Is Not

Using a Jowih service does not make you an owner of the Foundation. Integrating a business with Jowih does not give that business control of the Foundation. Investing in eligible commercial assets does not grant constitutional authority over the mission.

The mission layer is protected. The commercial layer is investable. The two are separated by design.

How the Fund Works

Capital allocation follows the independently established dollar value of qualifying assets vaulted — not the nominal equity percentage deposited. A 5% stake in a US$1B company may receive more allocation than a 49% stake in a US$20M company.

The Fund also includes a separately capped Neutral Reserve — up to 10% of total Fund NAV — for qualifying neutral assets such as land. Land in the Neutral Reserve appreciates when development happens around it. Because no new units are minted, the appreciation flows directly to NAV per unit. Unit holders' units appreciate without dilution.
