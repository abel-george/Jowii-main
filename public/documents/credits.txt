"Credits are a bridge — not a currency. They never enter the domestic economy. Auto-Conversion is default. Holding is optional. Partners convert. Foundation holds the bridge. Credits may be invisible to the customer (Credit-Routed FX), held as a balance (Holding Mode), or transferred directly (Wallet Transfer) — depending on the transaction mode. But they are never a parallel domestic currency."

"We specialize in the most active, daily, high-friction layer of global finance — and we make it move better. We create and aggregate high-frequency cross-border flows. Traditional FX settles what we create. Execution done by partners. Different roles. Same system."

4.2 Partner Flexibility

Partners are not restricted to a single tier. Large or hybrid partners may operate across both retail and wholesale channels. Partners may define their own operational thresholds and:

Execute transactions directly; or

Route flows to Tier 1 institutions based on capacity or strategy

4.3 Transaction Flow and Settlement Framework

The Jowih structure operates through a distributed partner network supported by a centralized settlement layer.

Participating partners — including institutional, wholesale, and hybrid operators — independently execute transactions based on their internal capabilities, external liquidity access, and operational models.

The Foundation does not directly intermediate or execute transactions. Instead, it provides the underlying settlement infrastructure that ensures system-wide consistency, reconciliation, and stability.

All transactions within the system are evaluated based on wallet currency state alignment, ensuring that any cross-currency movement of value is consistently identified and treated as an FX event regardless of transaction structure or routing path.

4.4 Partner Execution Model

Partners may process transactions through their own internal systems or in coordination with other network participants.

A. Internal Execution

Partners with sufficient external liquidity, infrastructure, and counterparty access may independently execute transactions within their own operational environment.

Under this model:

Transactions are processed entirely within the partner's internal system

The partner retains control over execution, pricing, and FX handling

Currency conversion (where applicable) is handled through the partner's own FX channels

No direct operational involvement from the Foundation is required during execution

The Foundation participates only at the settlement stage

B. Network-Enabled Execution

Where internal execution is not optimal, partners may coordinate with other participants or route transactions across the partner network.

Under this model:

Transactions are fulfilled through inter-partner collaboration

Execution responsibilities may be distributed across multiple entities

Partners maintain flexibility in sourcing external liquidity and counterparties

Cross-currency outcomes remain subject to FX classification based on wallet currency state mismatch

4.5 Partner Routing Framework

Partners may choose between two execution pathways:

1. Internal Execution:

Transactions are executed using the partner's own liquidity sources, banking relationships, or FX providers

Jowih Credits are used only for post-execution settlement recording

The partner retains full execution margin

2. Routed Execution:

Transactions are forwarded to another partner or Tier 1 institution

Execution occurs externally within the network or traditional FX markets

The originating partner earns a routing or facilitation margin

4.6 Mandatory System Rule

All transactions, regardless of execution path, must be recorded and reconciled through the Foundation's credit ledger. Final external settlement occurs in the applicable local or foreign currency through licensed execution partners.

This ensures:

A single, unified accounting layer

No bypass of system reconciliation

Consistent value representation across all participants

Preservation of system integrity without restricting execution flexibility

Additionally:

"Any transfer resulting in a currency difference between originating and receiving wallets is classified as an FX event, independent of execution path or transaction structure."

4.8 Non-Competition Commitment

The Foundation shall not operate retail or wholesale fiat on-ramp or off-ramp services in any market where licensed partners are actively offering conversion services.

The Foundation's role is limited to:

The bridge layer (Jowih Credits issuance, holding, and transfer)

The stable credit layer (treasury, reserve backing)

All conversions — retail and wholesale — are handled exclusively by partners.

Partners with wholesale capacity may handle conversions at their discretion. Partners may also route flows to Tier 1 institutions at their discretion.

This commitment ensures partners remain the default face of the Jowih Wallet and the backbone for all conversions. The Foundation is not a competitor — it is an enabler.

9. CREDIT-ROUTED FX — DEFINITION

Credit-Routed FX is the Foundation's FX routing mechanism through which Jowih Credits serve as the underlying routing unit between two currencies.

Under Credit-Routed FX, a currency conversion may be processed through the Jowih Credit infrastructure without requiring the customer to acquire or retain Jowih Credits as a visible balance.

The general routing structure is:

Currency A → Jowih Credit Routing Layer → Currency B

The customer-facing transaction may therefore begin and end in the respective local currencies, while Jowih Credits perform the underlying routing function within the system.

Credit-Routed FX is intended to provide an alternative to traditional FX execution for transactions within the applicable Jowih transaction ceiling ($200M per FX-classified transaction).

17. NEUTRALITY CLAUSE

"The Foundation shall not favor any currency, jurisdiction, partner, or financial system. All currencies, jurisdictions, partners, and financial systems shall be treated equally and without preference, subject only to regulatory compliance and operational capability. The Foundation shall not engage in currency speculation, market manipulation, or any activity that could be construed as favoring one currency or jurisdiction over another. This neutrality is fundamental to the Foundation's role as a neutral bridge between currencies and systems."
